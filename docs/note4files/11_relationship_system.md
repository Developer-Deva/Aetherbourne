## Overview
The Relationship System stores a creature’s long-term perception of other creatures.

Relationships are:

- persistent social structures
- developed through interaction, emotion, memory, and shared history

Relationships influence:

- social behavior
- courtship
- friendship
- parenting
- group formation
- trust
- conflict
- cooperation
- mate selection
- leadership
- memory interpretation

Important distinction:

- Relationships are **not** emotions.
- Emotions are temporary; relationships act as stable baselines.

## Simulation Role
Answers:

- **“What does this creature think about this other creature over a macro timeline?”**

## Processing Order / Update Cadence
The system updates when socially relevant events occur, with periodic maintenance:

1. **Event-driven updates**
   - Social Event → Relationship Evaluation → Relationship Modification → Memory Creation → Relationship State Update → Publish Relationship Outputs

2. **Periodic maintenance**
   - daily update → familiarity decay → attraction recalculation → loyalty recalculation

Gemini rewrite adds a two-speed cadence concept:

- real-time: reacts instantly to socially relevant action results / world events
- maintenance: runs once per day for stabilization and passive shifts

## Relationship Value Range
All relationship values use:

- -100 to +100

Meaning:

- -100 strong negative
- 0 neutral/unknown baseline
- +100 strong positive

Examples:
- Trust: -100 completely distrusted; 0 unknown; +100 completely trusted
- Affection: -100 strong dislike; 0 neutral; +100 deep affection

## Relationship Components (Meaning & Drivers)
### Familiarity (0..100)
- how well creatures know each other
- generated by time spent, conversations, shared activities, repeated encounters

Effects:
- prediction accuracy
- improves memory retrieval
- reduces social uncertainty

### Trust (-100..+100)
- belief in beneficial/predictable behavior

Increases from:
- help, honesty, reliability, protection

Decreases from:
- betrayal, theft, deception, abandonment

Effects:
- cooperation, trade willingness, group formation

### Affection
- fondness and attachment

Increases:
- positive interaction, support, shared experiences, caregiving

Decreases:
- neglect, rejection, abuse

Effects:
- bonding, friendship, family cohesion

### Attraction
- romantic/sexual interest

Increases from:
- physical preference, emotional compatibility, familiarity, affection

Decreases:
- rejection, incompatibility, rival bonding

Courtship availability can depend on attraction along with other thresholds.

### Respect
- admiration of competence/status/skill

Increases:
- success, leadership, expertise, strength

Decreases:
- failure, cowardice, incompetence

Effects:
- following, leadership acceptance, teaching effectiveness

### Loyalty
- commitment to maintaining a relationship

Increases:
- trust, shared history, affection, mutual support

Decreases:
- betrayal, prolonged absence, rival commitments

Effects:
- staying with groups, defending allies, relationship persistence

### Rivalry
- competitive opposition/friction

Increases:
- competition, repeated conflict, resource disputes, status contests

Effects:
- competition, challenge behavior, social tension

Can coexist with respect.

### Fear
- expectation of danger from another creature

Increases from:
- threats, violence, punishment

Effects:
- avoidance, submission, caution

Can coexist with loyalty.

### Dependency
- reliance mapping

Increases from:
- caregiving, protection, resource support

Effects:
- following, proximity seeking, separation distress

### Kinship
- family connection / clan marker

Usually generated from:

- parent-child relationships
- siblings
- clan structures
- adoption

Effects:
- increased helping
- parenting investment
- inheritance behavior

Generally stable.

## Relationship States (Derived Labels)
Labels are derived from values (not stored).

Examples present in notes:

- Stranger: familiarity < 20
- Acquaintance: familiarity ≥ 20
- Friend: affection > 50 AND trust > 40
- Close Friend: affection > 75 AND trust > 75
- Rival: rivalry > 50
- Enemy: trust < -50 AND affection < -50
- Partner: affection > 70 AND trust > 70 AND attraction > 50 (mutual bond established)
- Parent: kinship > 50 AND caregiver role active

## Courtship & Partnership (Behavior vs Relationship)
Courtship is a behavior strategy.

Courtship becomes available when:

- attraction exceeds threshold
- trust exceeds threshold
- affection exceeds threshold

Gemini rewrite defines a utility-like availability form:

- Courtship_Available = (Attraction > 50) AND (Trust > 40) AND (Affection > 40)

Courtship can involve actions:
- Speak, Give, Help, Praise, Bond

Courtship may produce a relationship state:
- Partnership (requires mutual attraction/trust/affection, and successful courtship)

Partnership increases:
- loyalty, dependency, parenting, cooperation

## Parenting (Behavior Strategy)
Parenting is a behavior strategy.

Parent-child relationships use:

- kinship, affection, trust, dependency

Parenting behaviors include:
- Help, Teach, Protect, Provide, Bond

## Relationship Growth Mechanics
Relationship changes are event-driven.

A general mutation approach is described:

- `ΔValue = E_Base × E_Intensity × P_Trait`

From the notes:
- E_Base: raw social value from action instance (e.g., Praise = +15 Respect)
- E_Intensity: emotional amplifier scaled from actor arousal/discrete state
- P_Trait: personality dispositions

## Personality & Emotion Effects on Relationship
Examples:

- Affiliation modifies affection gain and bond formation
- Empathy modifies trust growth and caregiving
- Contention amplifies rivalry growth and suppresses affection scaling

Emotion effects:
- Attachment increases affection, loyalty, dependency
- Anger decreases trust, increases rivalry
- Joy increases affection/trust
- Fear increases fear/dependency

## Memory Effects
Relationship updates should heavily use memory.

Examples:
- repeated help → trust growth
- repeated betrayal → trust loss
- repeated praise → respect growth
- repeated care → affection growth

## Design Goals
- persistent social modeling
- support friendship, rivalry, courtship, partnership, parenting
- separate emotions from relationships
- allow mixed relationships
- support emergent social structures
- scale efficiently to large populations
- meaningful inputs to Behavior, Emotion, Memory, Personality systems
