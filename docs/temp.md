Stats System

Description: Core attributes, derived competencies, and emergent behavioral capacities that influence perception, learning, decision-making, and long-term development in Aetherbourne.

Last Updated: 2026-06-26

---

Overview

The Stats System defines the fundamental capabilities of a creature.

Stats do not directly determine behavior.

Instead, stats determine what a creature is capable of perceiving, learning, enduring, understanding, and accomplishing.

The system is intentionally layered.

Core Stats
↓
Advanced Stats
↓
Emergent Stats
↓
Decision Making
↓
Experience
↓
Memory
↓
Personality Development

This structure allows creatures with similar genetics to develop into very different individuals through experience.

---

Design Philosophy

The Stats System exists to model capability rather than personality.

A creature's personality is shaped primarily through:

- Experience
- Memory
- Relationships
- Emotion
- Development

Stats influence how those experiences occur.

A strong creature may experience the world differently than a weak creature.

An observant creature may notice opportunities that others miss.

A determined creature may persist through hardships that cause others to abandon their goals.

The purpose of stats is to shape opportunity and capability rather than directly controlling behavior.

---

Layer Structure

The system is divided into three layers.

Core Stats
= Raw capabilities

Advanced Stats
= Broad competencies

Emergent Stats
= Behavioral capacities

---

Stat Lattice

The stat system is designed as a balanced lattice.

Every Core Stat contributes to exactly two Advanced Stats.

Every Advanced Stat contributes to exactly two Emergent Stats.

This ensures:

- No stat becomes a dead end.
- No stat dominates the system.
- Improvements ripple naturally through the hierarchy.
- Emergent behavior remains balanced.

---

Core Stats

Core Stats are the only permanent creature attributes that are directly stored.

These may be influenced by:

- Genetics
- Species
- Development
- Training
- Aging
- Injury
- Disease

---

Strength

Represents force production and physical power.

Primary uses:

- Carrying
- Mining
- Construction
- Melee combat
- Grappling
- Throwing
- Resource extraction

Questions it answers:

How much force can this creature generate?
How much weight can it move?

---

Stamina

Represents physical endurance and energy sustainability.

Primary uses:

- Travel
- Labor
- Hunting
- Recovery
- Fatigue resistance

Questions it answers:

How long can this creature continue performing?
How quickly does it tire?

---

Dexterity

Represents coordination, precision, and fine motor control.

Primary uses:

- Crafting
- Harvesting
- Tool use
- Accuracy
- Dodging
- Manipulation

Questions it answers:

How precisely can this creature act?
How well can it control movement?

---

Perception

Represents awareness and sensory capability.

Primary uses:

- Detection
- Tracking
- Observation
- Threat recognition
- Resource spotting
- Environmental awareness

Questions it answers:

What can this creature notice?
How much information can it acquire?

Important:

Perception does not guarantee awareness.

It determines what the creature is capable of sensing.

The Perception System determines whether the creature actually notices it.

---

Willpower

Represents mental persistence and self-control.

Primary uses:

- Goal commitment
- Emotional regulation
- Fear resistance
- Pain tolerance
- Long-term planning

Questions it answers:

How strongly can this creature maintain intention?
How resistant is it to giving up?

---

Advanced Stats

Advanced Stats are broad competencies derived from Core Stats.

They are not stored directly.

They are calculated dynamically.

---

Formulas

Endurance  = (Strength + Stamina) / 2
Prowess    = (Strength + Dexterity) / 2
Finesse    = (Dexterity + Perception) / 2
Conviction = (Willpower + Perception) / 2
Vitality   = (Stamina + Willpower) / 2

Emergent Stats

Emergent Stats are second-order derived values.

These should generally remain hidden from the player.

They are not skills.

They are not personality traits.

They are behavioral capacities that emerge from the interaction of broader competencies.

These act as a bridge between raw capability and long-term behavior.

Memory System

Description: Storage, retrieval, reinforcement, decay, and long-term influence of experience in Aetherbourne.

Last Updated: 2026-06-26

---

Overview

The Memory System stores significant experiences and learned information.

Memory serves as the creature's personal history.

It allows past experiences to influence future decisions, emotional responses, relationships, knowledge, skill development, and personality growth.

Memory is both an output and an input of the cognitive architecture.

Experiences create memories.

Memories influence future experiences.

---

Simulation Role

The Memory System answers:

«What has happened to me before?»

Memories influence:

- Decision making
- Emotional appraisal
- Relationship formation
- Skill development
- Knowledge acquisition
- Personality drift
- Goal selection
- Social behavior

Without memory, creatures can react but cannot learn.

---

Design Philosophy

Memory Is Selective

Not every event should be stored.

The simulation should remember what matters.

Important experiences are retained.

Insignificant experiences are discarded.

---

Memory Is Dynamic

Memories change over time.

They may:

- Strengthen
- Decay
- Be reinforced
- Be forgotten

Decision System

Description: Evaluates needs, motivations, emotions, personality, relationships, memories, and environmental context to select behaviors and actions.

Last Updated: 2026-06-26


---

Overview

The Decision System is the creature's executive layer.

Its purpose is to determine:

What the creature wants.

Which behavior should be active.

Which action should be performed next.

When plans should change.

When plans should continue.


The Decision System does not directly affect the world.

Instead, it selects behaviors and actions that will affect the world.


---

Simulation Role

The Decision System answers:

> What should I do next?



It sits between internal state and behavior execution.

---

Design Philosophy

Decisions Are Utility-Based

Creatures do not follow hardcoded scripts.

Instead, possible behaviors compete based on utility.

The most desirable option is selected.


---

Decisions Are Contextual

The same creature may choose different behaviors depending on:

Current needs

Current emotions

Relationships

Available opportunities

Environment

Recent memories



---

Decisions Are Imperfect

Creatures should not always choose the mathematically optimal option.

Personality, emotion, stress, fatigue, incomplete information, and memory bias should influence choices.


---

Decisions Operate Continuously

Creatures constantly reevaluate their situation.

However, reevaluation should not automatically cause behavior switching.

Commitment and inertia are important.
